Route Gen AI About Open the app

Routes in plain English

Tell it the ride you want — "a 40 mile loop from home, not much climbing" — and get a GPX file your Garmin or Wahoo can navigate, built on quiet roads cyclists actually want to ride.

What it does

  • Composes rides: loops or out-and-backs at your distance, flattest possible, max climbing (it finds real summits), or through towns you name.
  • Finds interval spots: a flat, uninterrupted stretch for 2×20 threshold work, or a steady grade for VO2 reps — scored against real stop signs and traffic lights from OpenStreetMap.
  • Edits any route conversationally: upload a GPX you already love, then "avoid that road", "add a stop at the café", "make it 10 miles longer", "start and end at my place". Changes splice locally; the rest of your route is untouched, and there's an undo.

How it works (and why the AI never draws the map)

A language model does exactly one job: reading your request into a precise, typed specification. Everything after that is deterministic — an open-source routing engine over OpenStreetMap data proposes candidates, and computed validators judge them: distance tolerance, climbing (calibrated against real head-unit measurements), how many meters land on busy highways, how much road repeats, whether an "avoid" actually cleared the road it named. Results are verified measurements, not model opinions — when an edit only partly works, the app says so.

Built in the open

The whole project — including a development log of every field-tested failure and fix — is open source: github.com/NKimball1/route-gen-ai.

Privacy

No accounts. Uploaded GPX files are normalized on arrival — timestamps and sensor data (heart rate, power) from ride recordings are stripped and never stored. Requests are logged to improve the tool.

Built in Madison, Wisconsin — field-tested on the roads it routes.
